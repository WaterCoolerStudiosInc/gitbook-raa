# Earning Yield

## Raa LSTs' reward-bearing design

Liquid Staking Tokens (LSTs) distribute staking rewards through two main approaches: **reward-bearing tokens** and **rebasing tokens**.

{% hint style="success" %}
sSTRK is a reward-bearing token.
{% endhint %}

Reward-bearing tokens maintain a fixed supply while their value grows through earned rewards. As staking rewards accumulate, each sToken token's value increases while your sToken token balance remains unchanged.

**Key Characteristics**:

1. **Fixed Supply**: Your token balance remains unchanged
2. **Value Growth**: Each sToken's value increases relative to its corresponding Gas Token with accumulated rewards
3. **Composability:** Since the supply is fixed, sTokens offer superior integration capabilities with DeFi protocols across the ecosystem.

### Rebasing Tokens

Rebasing tokens automatically adjust their total supply to reflect earned rewards and market conditions. When the protocol earns staking rewards, it increases the token supply proportionally, automatically crediting additional tokens to holders' wallets without requiring new deposits. The downside of this is that rebasing tokens cannot be easily integrated into DeFi protocols, because their supply is not fixed. Raa therefore does not use this model.

## sSTRK Yield Example

Each [Era](definitions.md), there is a certain fixed amount of yield generated by the network in the form of newly generated STRK tokens. The rate of yield is set by the network.

The yield accrues to the Validators, which must return it back to the Vault. This part is enforced by a mixture of Raa's smart contracts and blockchain-native mechanisms.

However, while the number of STRK in the Raa staking pool (the Vault) increases, the number of outstanding sSTRK does not. Remember, sSTRK represent a share of the Total Pool of STRK ([`totalPooled`](definitions.md)) controlled by the Vault. So, when the user redeems their sSTRK for STRK, they will receive more STRK than they originally deposited.

{% hint style="info" %}
Here's a \*_very\*_ simple example:

1. At the beginning the Vault contains 0 STRK (`totalPooled` = 0) and there are 0 sSTRK outstanding (`totalShares` = 0)
2. A user deposits (stakes) 10 STRK. The Vault mints 10 sSTRK and transfers them to the user.
3. Now, the user waits. Over a period of time, the staked STRK accrue yield in the form of additional STRK.
   1. Let's say for simplicity that the tokens accrued a 10% yield over that time.Comment
   2. Let's also say for simplicity's sake that no other users deposit any STRK for staking, and of course no other user redeems any tokens.
4. At this point, the `totalShares` still = 10 sSTRK. However, due to the 10% yield accrued, the `totalPooled` now equals 11 STRK.
5. Now, the user redeems all 10 of their sSTRK for STRK. They receive 11 STRK in return.
   1. Note that after this step, the Vault's `totalShares` = 0 again and `totalPooled` = 0 again as well.
{% endhint %}

This was a very simplified example. It doesn't account for any Protocol fees, and it doesn't account for other users staking and unstaking their tokens over time. However, it illustrates well the mechanism by which Raa helps users generate yield on their tokens. The example shows how yield came from the network's natural rate of STRK inflation, and Raa helped the user access it easily.

## Composable Yield

Raa's sTokens are fungible, and therefore allow users to participate in other network activities, such as DeFi. Many DeFi products allow users to earn additional yield through processes of lending, trading, and more. So, users who use sTokens to earn yield in DeFi can _also_ take advantage of the network's natural staking yield at the same time. This is what we call composable yield.
